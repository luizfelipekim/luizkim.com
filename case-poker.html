Context

Low UX maturity, high product risk

No user testing, no real personas. Decisions ran on assumptions, the product had drifted from what players needed, and product didn't trust UX. The business wanted more casual players, which meant knowing who they were.

Key decisions and what each one cost

Personas from real players, not workshops

Moderated interviews with a mix of players: testing-panel users, professionals, high-stakes players and streamers. Then a standing focus group.

Test the competitor before designing ours

Moderated sessions on a competitor's hand history and hand-strength meter, so our version started from evidence.

What it took: a dedicated researcher, so we could test before the sprint started.

Challenge the industry's avatars

Poker avatars are usually exaggerated and stereotyped. Players preferred realistic, diverse ones, and that's what shipped.

Traded off: the look the industry was used to. Product pushed back, then agreed once they heard players.

Let stakeholders watch players

Most of the product team doubted research. Instead of arguing in decks, I brought them into the persona studies to watch real players. It became a habit: whenever design or product wasn't sure, we ran a usability test to back the decision.

Who did the research

9 designers, 1 dedicated researcher and 1 UX writer. Participants: testing-panel users, professional players, high-stakes players and streamers.

The avatar study

In moderated sessions, 20 of 24 players (83%) preferred realistic, diverse avatars. 4 (17%) preferred the cartoon style the industry uses. The realistic set shipped.

To a peer

Get better artists in earlier

The research was right, but the graphics took too much back and forth. Next time I'd bring in stronger artists from the start.
