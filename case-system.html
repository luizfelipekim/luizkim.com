Context

The business problem

40+ brands, three product areas, six regions: the US, Canada, LATAM, Iberia, the UK and Germany. Teams worked in silos and duplicated work. Rollouts were slow and brands drifted apart. An older system existed, but a tech-stack change meant starting again from scratch.

Key decisions and what each one cost

Run the design system as a product

No one owned the system beyond shipping components. I set up the process: investigate, design, build, integrate.

Traded off: every product team had its own way of working, so each new PM needed onboarding, and the process kept adapting as we went.

Decentralise ownership

Product and market experts owned their slice; DesignOps owned the structure.

Traded off: less central control, more maintenance, fewer components built for one product.

Self-serve, not support tickets

Figma, Storybook, zeroheight, Jira and a video library, so teams helped themselves and DesignOps focused on delivery.

Who pushed back

Teams in emerging global markets. They needed speed and flexibility, and sometimes their needs were different from everyone else's. We brought them closer to the system.

How ownership was split

DesignOps held the structure: tokens, governance, the toolchain and the reports. Eight ambassadors, some product-specific and some market-specific, owned their slice of the system and brought local needs back into it. Teams served themselves from Figma, Storybook, zeroheight and a curated video library instead of raising tickets.
