February 2026 survey of both teams.

Atomic AI adoption, in four phases

One rule: give designers the leverage engineering had, then let them build tools for their own pain.

  1. March Access VS Code, Git and Claude Code for every designer. Weekly sessions on GitHub, branches and pull requests. First commit 20 March. Four designers committing within four weeks.
  2. April to June Atoms One pain point, one slash command: find-figma, figma-annotate, design-qa, and bind-tokens. 9,658 token bindings, zero errors, in a designer's first pull request.
  3. July to August Pipeline Commands chained into an 8-stage pipeline, intake to handoff, taught through an Education Hub. Then two Figma plugins and an intake bot. 36 tools, three languages.
  4. September to October Production Ship to engineering from the prototype; Figma polish in parallel. A bridge imports prototypes into Figma as real library instances. Home, PKO and Leaderboards shipped from prototypes.

The foundation under the pipeline

Key decisions and what each one cost

Skills first, the agent last

I built a UX orchestrator agent in March, without asking designers what they needed. I shelved it and inverted the order.

Traded off: our most demo-able thing, for small tools designers asked for.

Every tool earns its place

Every tool ends in adopt, adopt-with-limits or drop, backed by a written pilot evaluation, scorecards and synthetic-persona runs.

Traded off: speed. The Claude Design pilot: 316 prompts, 13 to 16 hours, one ticket; 8% of prompts decided the design. It became the exploration tool, not the handoff.

Prototype-first delivery

From 15 September, prototypes go to engineering; Figma polish runs in parallel.

Traded off: Figma is no longer the source at handoff.

Turning a critical review into owned fixes

In October the product lead listed what was still broken. Each flaw got a mechanism, an owner and a status.

  • Fixed

    Prototypes looked like the product but weren't made of it

    bind-tokens, an adherence plugin that scores and rebinds, and the Figma bridge. Off the friction list.

  • Mechanism live

    Design brought in late; UI shipped without design

    July: 11 of 79 stories had a design link. Now "Design required" creates a design ticket, and Done fires a Design QA ticket.

  • Baseline set

    No baselines to measure against

    Time and iteration tracking since 21 Sep, a weekly KPI snapshot, a 25-ticket Jira study.

  • Designed, not shipped

    Product flaws found by research

    A silent 7 to 20 minute import, hidden filters and a dead-end "Skip for now" went into the Home and import redesigns.

  • Q4

    Design tokens reach the frontend by hand

    Token architecture proven on a library copy; Figma-to-pull-request pipeline proven in August. Live migration is the Q4 item.

  • Open

    Communication with engineering after handoff

    Owners named. Engineering still reports no change; a buddy developer per designer is proposed.

What research found

First moderated study: 0 of 5 participants reached their analysis unassisted. Synthetic personas: 0 of 4 understood the progress block. Both fed the Home and import redesigns.

5 moderated sessions, August · persona runs, September

To a peer

Tooling does nothing on its own; adoption is the work

Adoption was uneven. One designer wrote 28 of 37 commands; one opened 43 pull requests, 37 merged. Weekly sessions made the first commit possible, a few hands made the volume, and now every designer ships from prototypes. The lesson: ask designers what hurts before building for them.

Not proven yet

  • Hours saved: not measured. One scorecard filled; target is 80% of runs.
  • Tickets are counts, not effort: sizing at pickup is 50 to 67%.
  • Design tickets at Ready for Dev: 10 in Q3, 8 in early October. Promising, small.
  • Credit limits and prototype hallucinations still interrupt work.

Q4 asks: token auto-sync with an engineering owner, a buddy developer per designer, the AI design-engineering hire.
